Plan the shortest delivery round.

DropLine takes a depot, a set of customers and a city full of traffic, then finds the cheapest order to visit them all.

Dijkstra measures the roadsGreedy builds a quick routeBacktracking finds the best
1

The problem

A rider starts at a source, must visit every customer once, and finishes at a destination (or returns to the source). Roads have traffic costs and some are closed. Which visiting order costs least?

Input

A weighted city grid (traffic cost 1 to 5 per junction, closed roads), a source, a destination and k customer stops.

Output

The visiting order, the road path between stops, and the total cost.

Dijkstra

Finds the cheapest road path between every pair of stops and builds the cost table.

Greedy

Always drives to the nearest unvisited stop. Very fast, but can be wrong.

Backtracking

Tries visiting orders and drops a partial route once it costs more than the best found. Always optimal.

Why these algorithms?

Dijkstra suits the road layer because traffic costs are never negative. It runs once from the depot and once from each stop, giving a (k+1)×(k+1) cost table. Greedy is short-sighted: an early cheap move can force a long trip later, as test 5 shows. Backtracking abandons a partial route as soon as its cost reaches the best complete route found so far. Seeding that limit with the greedy answer prunes most of the search, and the result is exact.

Alternatives: dynamic programming (Held-Karp, O(2kk²)) is faster for larger k but needs more memory. Genetic algorithms and 2-opt give good, not guaranteed, routes for hundreds of stops. For a rider with under about 12 stops, pruned backtracking is simple and exact.

Practical use and limits

The same pipeline suits food delivery, courier rounds, garbage collection and field-service visits: swap the grid for a real road graph and refresh costs from live traffic. Limits: the grid ignores one-way streets and delivery time windows, and exact backtracking stops being practical beyond roughly 12 stops, where Held-Karp or a heuristic takes over.
